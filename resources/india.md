# Getting Hardware in India

Treat a listing as a source, not as proof that a part is suitable. Check the manufacturer, exact part number, datasheet, package, pinout, stock status, invoice, return window, and seller information. For safety-critical or expensive parts, prefer an authorized distributor.

## Online sources

- [Element14 / Farnell](https://in.element14.com/): broad catalogue, manufacturer documentation, and professional procurement workflows.
- [Mouser India](https://www.mouser.in/): parametric search for ICs, connectors, semiconductors, and development hardware.
- [DigiKey India](https://www.digikey.in/): large catalogue, technical data, manufacturer traceability information, and India ordering support.
- [ElectronicsComp](https://www.electronicscomp.com/): Indian source for common components, modules, tools, and prototyping quantities. Check the listing and returns for each order.
- [Robu](https://robu.in/): development boards, sensors, robotics parts, tools, batteries, and services including PCB manufacturing.
- [Robomart](https://robomart.com/): maker and robotics parts, common ICs, modules, wires, and tools.
- [Electronify India](https://www.electronifyindia.com/): components, connectors, controller ICs, modules, and quotation support.

No seller is a substitute for a datasheet. Compare equivalent part numbers carefully, especially for voltage regulators, MOSFETs, sensors, batteries, and USB or CAN interface parts.

## Delhi NCR

- **Lajpat Rai Market, Chandni Chowk:** a component market useful for common parts, connectors, wires, tools, and repair-oriented sourcing. Inspect the part and ask for a bill and return terms.
- **Nehru Place:** a large electronics and computer market. It is useful for computer-related hardware, adapters, repair work, and finding component or service leads, but individual shops vary.
- **[Robosaki](https://robosaki.com/):** a Delhi/NCR robotics and IoT parts seller for boards, sensors, motors, drivers, batteries, and tools. Confirm the current pickup address and return terms before travelling or ordering.
- **[Maker Junction](https://makerjunction.in/):** a 3D-printer, filament, and rapid-prototyping supplier. Treat it as a prototyping service and maker supplier, not as a general IC distributor.

These are markets, not a guarantee of authorized distribution. They should be distinguished from:

- **Component markets:** buy common parts and ask for identification, packing, and a bill.
- **Repair markets:** useful for diagnosis, salvage, and practical advice, but salvaged parts need testing.
- **Prototyping services:** useful for 3D printing, laser cutting, assembly, or small mechanical parts.
- **PCB services:** useful for fabrication and assembly from your Gerbers and BOM. Confirm stackup, minimum rules, shipping, inspection, and rework policy.

Business names and stall locations change. Verify current opening hours, address, and service scope before travelling. This repository does not certify any shop or claim that any market is an authorized source.

## Inspecting informal-market parts

Look for suspicious package markings, inconsistent manufacturer markings, damaged pins, oxidation, unusual or mismatched packaging, and prices that are implausibly low. Compare the package and pinout with the manufacturer datasheet. Test simple components when practical and use a known-good circuit for comparison. Visual inspection can find warning signs, but it cannot prove an IC is genuine. Buy critical parts from a reputable distributor and keep the invoice and lot information.
