# Tools

Buy tools when a project needs them. A basic workbench is enough for Levels 0 and 1.

## Start here

- **Digital multimeter:** measure voltage, resistance, continuity, diode drop, and current when connected correctly.
- **Breadboard:** prototype low-voltage, low-current circuits without soldering. It is not a power or high-frequency interconnect.
- **Jumper wires:** make temporary connections. Keep colours consistent so debugging stays possible.
- **Basic hand tools:** flush cutters, wire stripper, small pliers, tweezers, and precision screwdrivers help prepare wires, hold parts, and open enclosures.

## Add for soldered prototypes

- **Soldering iron or station:** make permanent electrical and mechanical joints. A temperature-controlled entry-level iron is enough.
- **Solder:** choose a suitable electronics solder and use ventilation. Leaded solder is easier for learning but needs sensible hygiene; follow local disposal rules.
- **Desoldering pump and solder wick:** remove through-hole solder and clean bridges.
- **Crocodile clips:** make temporary meter or supply connections, while avoiding accidental shorts.
- **Helping hands or clamps:** hold a board and wire safely while soldering.

A TS100 is an example of a portable affordable iron; a Hakko FX-951 is a professional option, not a beginner requirement. Verify current availability and the seller before buying.

A normal silicone soldering mat protects a table from heat and solder. It is not automatically an ESD mat. Proper ESD equipment uses a dissipative surface, a suitable ground connection, and a correctly fitted wrist strap or other control method. Do not connect an improvised ground to mains earth without understanding the safety requirements.
